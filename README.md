_**Fig. 1 | Rapamycin inhibits mTORC1 and suppresses the senescence-associated secretory phenotype.**_ Rapamycin binds the intracellular immunophilin FKBP12; the resulting FKBP12–rapamycin complex engages the FRB domain of mTOR (PDB 1FAP), allosterically inhibiting mTORC1 (mTOR–Raptor–mLST8; PDB 6BCX). Loss of mTORC1 activity reduces p70S6K and 4E-BP1 phosphorylation, promotes autophagy, and imposes a G1 cell-cycle arrest. In senescent cells, mTORC1 inhibition attenuates translation of SASP components and NF-κB-driven inflammatory signalling, lowering secretion of IL-6, IL-1α, IL-8 and MMP-3 (right). Chemical structure drawn from canonical SMILES; protein structures rendered from the indicated PDB entries; schematic created with BioRender.com.

_**Fig. 2 | β-Galactosidase-targeted activation of the senolytic prodrug SSK1.**_ SSK1 comprises gemcitabine linked through a self-immolative 4-(hydroxymethyl)-2-nitrophenyl spacer to a tetra-O-acetyl-β-D-galactopyranoside trigger. In non-senescent cells (left), low lysosomal β-galactosidase activity leaves SSK1 intact and essentially non-cytotoxic. In senescent cells (right), elevated senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) hydrolyses the O-glycosidic bond; the resulting phenolate undergoes 1,6-elimination with loss of CO₂, releasing cytotoxic gemcitabine together with the inert byproduct 4-(hydroxymethyl)-2-nitrophenol and D-galactose. Gemcitabine triggers p38 MAPK activation and apoptosis, selectively eliminating SA-β-gal-positive cells. Chemical structures were drawn from canonical SMILES; cell schematics created with BioRender.com.
